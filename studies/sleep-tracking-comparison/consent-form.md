## An invitation
You are being asked to take part in a research study. Taking part is **completely your
choice**. This form explains what the study involves so you can decide. Please ask us
anything that is unclear before you sign.

## Why we're doing this study
Many people use smartwatches like the Apple Watch and the WHOOP band to track their
sleep, but it isn't clear how accurate they are. We want to compare what these two
devices report against a **sleep-lab recording (polysomnography, or "PSG")**, which is
the medical standard for measuring sleep. Our study is **independent** — it is **not**
paid for by Apple, WHOOP, or any device company.

## Why you're being asked
You are being invited because you are an adult willing to spend **one night** in the
sleep lab. If you are a staff member or clinician: your participation is **voluntary**,
and choosing not to take part — or stopping at any time — will **not** affect your job,
evaluations, call schedule, or standing in any way.

## What you will be asked to do
If you agree, for **one night** you will:
- Have standard sleep-lab sensors placed on your scalp, face, chin, chest, and a finger
  (these record brain activity, eye movements, muscle tone, heart rhythm, breathing, and
  oxygen). This is the same setup used for a routine clinical sleep study.
- Wear **two wrist devices** at the same time — an **Apple Watch** on one wrist and a
  **WHOOP** on the other (we will decide which wrist by chance).
- Sleep in the lab overnight while everything records.
- In the morning, we remove the sensors and devices and collect the data.

You do not need to do anything special during the night except sleep as usual.

## How long it will take
One overnight visit (about `[X]` hours, evening to morning). Later, several trained
readers will review your (de-identified) recording — this does not involve you.

## Risks and discomforts
- **Skin irritation:** the sensor gel/adhesive can irritate the skin, and removing it
  (especially the scalp glue) can be uncomfortable or briefly unpleasant.
- **Poorer sleep:** you may sleep less well than at home the first night in a lab.
- **Privacy:** your health information could be seen by someone not authorized. We
  protect against this (see "Your privacy").
- These risks are **small** and similar to a routine clinical sleep study. Wearing the
  two watches adds essentially no risk.

## If we find something unexpected
The recording is for research, not diagnosis. But if a reviewer notices something that
looks **medically important** (for example, signs of sleep apnea, an irregular heartbeat,
or unusual brain-wave activity), a qualified clinician will review it and we will **tell
you** and suggest you follow up with an appropriate doctor. A normal-looking study does
**not** rule out a sleep or medical condition.

## Benefits
You may not get any direct medical benefit. If we happen to find something important, it
may help you seek care. The main benefit is to **others** — better independent
information about how much to trust smartwatch sleep data.

## Your privacy and how your data is used
- Your recording and device data are labeled with a **code**, not your name. The list
  linking the code to you is kept separately and securely, seen only by the study team.
- The people who read your sleep recording will **not** know who you are or what the
  watches reported.
- We keep the data on secure, approved systems for `[period]`, then dispose of it per
  policy.
- Results may be **published or presented**, but only in a way that does **not identify
  you** (grouped or example data). `[Insert institution HIPAA authorization language.]`
